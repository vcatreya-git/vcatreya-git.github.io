Re: Product Manager, Connect Platform — Connect Abstractions

Connect’s bet is that the highest-leverage financial infrastructure is what you build for platforms — so marketplaces, networks, and embedded-finance products can represent participants, onboard them safely, configure capabilities, and move money without rebuilding payments and compliance themselves. That is the layer I have worked on for 15 years: platform primitives that fintechs and multi-party operators depend on when the alternative is custom integration forever.

At FIS I own Open Access Connect, the FDX-aligned open-banking platform for fintech aggregators. The hard problem was never “expose account data.” It was: when a fintech wants to serve users across bank clients, how do you model that third party’s relationship with each bank — identity, authorized applications, data scope, consent lifecycle, and entitlement checks on every call — so the fintech onboards once and reuses a clean capability surface? We shipped that as a product abstraction, including a consent dashboard that works the same way across retail and business channels rather than as forked custom work per relationship. That is the shape of work I want to do on Connect accounts: start from a real platform use case, then harden the primitive.

The same instinct shows up elsewhere. The Money Movement Hub put one ISO 20022-aligned origination contract in front of wires, RTP, and FedNow so posting, balances, liens, and validation behaved the same way for integrators regardless of rail. The Notification Platform consolidates fragmented delivery vendors behind a single multi-tenant outbound API, with a roadmap that keeps early integrators stable while the platform absorbs more complexity (routing, failover, preferences, branding). On the payments side, I rebuilt a troubled multi-country hub at Qatar National Bank (5 countries, ~60% STP via intelligent correspondent routing, IBS Fintech Innovation Award 2019) and fed recurring custom gaps into base product so they stopped being one-off projects — the productising-bespoke-to- platform pattern Connect needs when platform demand gets ahead of the shared model.

What draws me to Connect Abstractions specifically is work that compounds: primitives that stay simple by default, stay flexible when platforms get weird, and do not fork every time a business model shifts. AI makes that more urgent, not less — more dynamic participants, multi-role operators, and non-human callers still need trustworthy onboarding, configurable capabilities, and clean lifecycle without re-onboarding theatre. Those are abstraction problems. I want to help define them at Stripe scale, for the next generation of platforms that will depend on Connect.

I would welcome the conversation.
