Re: Product Manager, Connect Platform — Connect Abstractions

Connect builds the infrastructure that platforms depend on — not direct to end-users, but for the builders who serve them. That architecture is not a new idea for me. For 15 years I have worked on exactly that layer of the stack: the platform primitives that fintechs, corporate channels, and multi-market payment operations have depended on to access financial capabilities without building the underlying systems themselves.

At FIS, I owned Open Access Connect — the FDX-aligned platform layer that let fintech aggregators onboard, establish consent, and access account data across bank clients. The product was not a data feed; it was an abstraction for a third party's relationship with a bank — their identity, their authorized applications, their permitted data scope, and the entitlement checks that governed every call. A fintech could onboard once, configure its capability scope per data type, and serve accounts across its entire network — with a self-service consent dashboard that worked identically across retail and business channels without a custom bank integration per relationship. That is the same problem Connect's account model is built to solve: represent participants, configure capabilities, manage lifecycle cleanly.

The same design instinct runs through the rest of my work at FIS. The Money Movement Hub placed a single ISO 20022-aligned contract in front of wire, RTP, and FedNow origination — bank client integrations to posting, balances, liens, and account validation worked identically regardless of rail. The Notification Platform I am currently roadmapping takes the same approach at the delivery layer: a unified outbound API today, evolving toward event-driven architecture that receives upstream events and generates bespoke end-customer notifications per channel preference, consent, and client branding — without integrators needing to reason about any of that complexity. On payments domain depth: a five-country hub at Qatar National Bank (~60% STP improvement via intelligent intermediary routing, IBS Fintech Innovation Award 2019) and multi-format payments transformation across 40+ markets at Standard Chartered. When the JD describes coordinating money movement across large networks, I have worked at every layer of what that actually requires.

What draws me to Connect Abstractions is the "abstractions that compound" problem — where a well-designed primitive extends cleanly as platforms grow and combine, rather than forking every time requirements shift. The most durable work I have done has been patterns: the consent architecture that served retail and business without forking; the multi-entity payments model at QNB that reached five countries with minimal rework. I also think about the AI dimension here not as a toolset but as what it demands from platform infrastructure — participants with flexible roles, capabilities configurable without re-onboarding, and trust models that work for non-human callers. Those are abstraction problems, not feature problems, and Connect Abstractions is the right place to define them.

I would welcome the conversation.
