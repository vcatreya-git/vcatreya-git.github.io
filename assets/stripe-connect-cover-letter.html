Re: Product Manager, Connect Platform — Connect Abstractions

Connect is Stripe's bet that the most valuable financial infrastructure is not what you build for end-users directly — it is what you build for the platforms that serve them. That design philosophy is not abstract to me. I have spent 15 years building exactly that kind of infrastructure, from the bank side: the platform primitives that fintechs, corporate channels, and multi-market payment operations depend on to function without having to build financial-services systems themselves.

At FIS, I owned Open Access Connect — the FDX-aligned platform layer that let fintech aggregators onboard, establish consent, and access account data across bank clients. The product was not a data feed; it was an abstraction for representing a third-party's relationship with a bank — their identity, their authorized applications, their permitted data scope, and the entitlement checks that governed every call. That is the same problem the Connect account model solves: represent participants, configure their capabilities, and manage their lifecycle cleanly across a platform's entire user base.

I have built multi-tenant platform primitives designed to absorb complexity rather than expose it. The Notification Platform I defined replaced fragmented SMS, email, and push vendors with a single outbound API — one integration contract for any bank client, regardless of channel or underlying provider, with priority routing, failover, and a full audit trail. The Money Movement Hub abstracted payment origination across wires, RTP, and FedNow behind ISO 20022-aligned contracts, so that core integrations — transaction posting, balance checks, lien placement, account validation — worked the same way regardless of rail. These are the same architectural moves Connect makes: one abstraction that holds across the diversity of what platforms need, today and as they grow.

On the payments domain: I have spent a decade in the mechanics of money movement — ISO 20022, real-time payments (RTP/FedNow), wires, ACH, correspondent routing, and multi-entity clearing — across 40+ markets at Standard Chartered and a five-country payments hub at Qatar National Bank (IBS Fintech Innovation Award 2019, ~60% STP improvement via intelligent intermediary routing). When the JD describes Connect helping platforms "coordinate money movement across large networks," I understand every layer of what that requires to actually work.

The AI dimension in the JD resonates with something I am already doing, not just observing. I use Claude Code as a development co-pilot to build production products from PRD to deployed code — PMRocket (a PM career navigation platform with RAG gap analysis against real job descriptions, pgvector HNSW indexing, and a five-stage intelligence pipeline) and Predictopia (a full-stack IPL prediction platform built solo in a nine-day sprint). Building with AI gives me a practitioner's view on what AI-native platforms actually need from financial infrastructure: flexible participant models, capability configuration that does not require re-onboarding, and APIs that are designed for agents as callers, not just humans. That is not a hypothetical for me — it is what I am already thinking about.

What draws me to this role specifically is the "abstractions that compound" framing. The most durable work I have done is not features — it is patterns. The channel-agnostic API design that let BPS serve retail and corporate without forking; the consent architecture that worked across Digital One Retail and Business; the multi-entity payments model at QNB that extended from Qatar to Singapore to the UK with minimal rework. Connect Abstractions is that kind of work, at Stripe scale and at the moment when AI is creating the next wave of platforms that will depend on it. I want to be part of defining what those abstractions look like.

I would welcome the conversation.
