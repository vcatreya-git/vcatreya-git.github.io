The Problem

17–35 hours of unpaid seasonal admin work

Every IPL season, someone in the family group becomes an unpaid administrator. They collect predictions over WhatsApp, enter them into a Google Sheet with colour-coded rows, tally scores after each match, manage payments, and remind everyone who hasn't predicted yet. Over 70+ matches, that's a second job — and if they burn out, the pool dies.

The core insight: the problem isn't that nobody has built a prediction app. It's that existing tools require setup by someone technical, don't handle offline money flows, and weren't designed for a family WhatsApp group. Predictopia is built for real ops — the ones that actually happen in Indian family sports groups.
📊

Manual score tallying

15–30 minutes per match day entering predictions, checking results, and updating the sheet. One person carries the load for 70+ matches.

💬

WhatsApp prediction chaos

Messages flood the group before each match. Late predictions, changed picks, and "I sent mine before toss" disputes are impossible to audit.

💸

Offline money friction

UPI and cash payments arrive at different times. Tracking who paid, who's approved, and who owes what requires a separate system from the predictions.

🔗

Per-match link spam

Admins send a new predictions link before every match. Members miss predictions when the link is buried in chat. Reminders add to the noise.

User Journeys

Six journeys. One product truth.

Persona-driven narratives — every feature maps to a named person with a concrete seasonal job. The journeys also function as a scope defence: features not serving these six scenarios were explicitly deferred.

Journey 1: Admin sets up the family IPL pool (Day 0)

Venkat — pool organiser — reducing 17–35 hours of unpaid seasonal labour to zero

Venkat organises the family WhatsApp cricket league every year. Historically he spends 15–30 minutes per match day on Google Sheets — entry columns, coloured cells, reminder messages, manual score tallies. Over 70+ IPL matches that is 17–35 hours of unpaid seasonal work. If he burns out mid-season, the pool dies. He is also the distribution channel — he creates pools and invites members.

He signs in with Google (no new password — family will not install a separate app). Onboarding takes three beats: display name, emoji avatar, favourite IPL team. The favourite team tints the nav accent so the app feels personal on first open.

He creates "Family IPL 2026" with ₹500 buy-in, 10% commission (admin-only, never shown to members), Top 3 distribution. As he adjusts expected headcount, the live prize preview recalculates exact amounts — answering the WhatsApp question that used to dominate setup night. He copies the permanent join link and pins it once in the family group. Setup done.

  • Product decisions visible: One permanent link eliminates per-match spam · Prize preview before first join reduces money confusion · Commission never shown to members avoids "tax" framing fights · Money stays offline eliminates regulatory complexity
Success metric: Pool live + link shared in under 10 minutes. Admin labour for the rest of the season collapses to approvals and occasional share nudges.

Journey 2: Casual uncle joins and makes his first pick (Match day 1)

Ramesh, 58 — wants to play when reminded, not learn a fintech app

Ramesh reads WhatsApp every day but will abandon anything with small text or multi-step forms. He taps the pinned link on his phone. The pool preview shows name, buy-in, and member count — not a full ranked leaderboard (showing a table of cousins already predicting would discourage him).

He requests to join. That evening Venkat approves and marks Paid separately after UPI arrives — two states, because real money is asynchronous. Ramesh opens Predict: large team tiles in CSK gold and MI blue, no copyrighted logos. One tap for his pick. Countdown: "Closes in 2h 15m." He changes his mind once; last pick sticks. After cutoff he sees the pool-wide reveal — banter content, not a spreadsheet.

  • Product decisions visible: 48px tap targets and plain language for seniors · Approve ≠ Paid state machine for offline UPI lag · Reveal only after cutoff (independent thinking, then banter) · No tie option matches IPL reality · Never show net losses to members
Success metric: First pick made before cutoff without calling Venkat for help.

Journey 3: Power user across two pools (All season)

Priya — family pool and college friends pool, different strategies per social graph

Priya is in two pools: family WhatsApp and a college friends group. My Pools shows both cards with role badges. For the same CSK vs MI fixture, she picks CSK in family pool and MI in college — different bragging rights, different social graph. Streaks and achievements are per-pool. "Perfect Week" in college does not pollute family standings.

She shares a leaderboard card to Instagram Stories. The card embeds a join QR — every screenshot is simultaneously an invite. Distribution designed into UX, not bolted on as a marketing feature.

  • Portfolio evidence: UNIQUE(pool_id, user_id, match_id) is a schema requirement, not an afterthought — multi-tenant prediction isolation at family scale.
Success metric: Two independent predictions for the same match, zero confusion about "which pool am I in?"

Journey 4: Mid-season admin ops (low touch)

Venkat again, week 6 — the product promise is tested here

Six weeks in, the V1 promise is under test: if Venkat is back in Sheets, the product failed. On a typical match day: he approves one pending join (rejects another — pool near the 100-member cap). He marks three members Paid after weekend UPI. He shares "Who hasn't predicted" into WhatsApp — three people tap through before cutoff.

He does not enter scores. The first person who opens the completed match card triggers the CricketData API call. ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING — one external call per match, served from cache for all subsequent users. Venkat checks the budget tracker: total collected, allocated, remaining — commission % stays admin-only.

  • Admin labour collapses to: Approvals + payment marking + occasional share nudges. Results, scoring, and leaderboard updates are zero-touch. API thrift is product strategy shaped by free-tier constraints.
Success metric: Match-day admin time under 5 minutes. Zero spreadsheet opens.

Journey 5: Season-end prizes (offline money, in-app truth)

Family after the final — offline UPI, in-app settlement record

Season leaderboard freezes after the final. The prize engine shows Top 3 amounts from the Nx formula: 10 paid members × ₹500 buy-in, 10% commission → ₹4,500 prize pool, 6 parts → 1st ₹2,250, 2nd ₹1,500, 3rd ₹750. If 1st and 2nd tie: they split (₹2,250+₹1,500)/2 = ₹1,875 each; rank inversion guard ensures no lower rank earns more after multi-way splits.

Venkat pays via UPI offline and marks prize rows Paid in app. Members see "Won ₹X" only — never a net-loss view. Buy-in is a sunk entry fee; showing losses discourages next season.

  • Boundary decision: App tracks prize settlement, does not process payments. No payment rails, no KYC, no regulatory risk. Knowing what NOT to build is the portfolio skill here.
Success metric: Paid marks match offline UPI. Zero "the app stole my money" support drama.

Journey 6: Late join (fairness without refunds)

Arjun joins mid-season — explicit policy, no contested logic

Arjun joins after 20 completed matches. He sees the buy-in upfront and requests to join. On approval and payment, the late-join policy applies: late joiners start from current fixtures, buy-in is still the full amount, past points are not backdated. Mid-season exit: buy-in stays in the pool with no refund, predictions preserved for history, points frozen from exit date. Contested refund logic is out of scope — clear policy, zero ambiguity.

Success metric: Arjun joins without a WhatsApp argument about what he should have scored in week one.

Technical Depth

Six deep-dives worth reading

The technical decisions that carry portfolio weight — a production RLS incident, architectural constraints as product strategy, and scope calls grounded in engineering reality.

RLS Nuclear Fix · Migration 007

Infinite recursion on pool_members

-- Original broken policy (simplified): -- pool_members SELECT: WHERE pool_id IN -- (SELECT pool_id FROM pool_members -- WHERE user_id = auth.uid()) -- → Policy queries the table it guards → ∞ -- Migration 007 core principles: -- 1. No table's policy ever queries itself -- 2. pool_members never queries pool_members -- 3. pools never queries pool_members -- Fix: self-access = auth.uid() = user_id -- Creator check = pools.created_by = auth.uid()

Early RLS policies let pool_members SELECT verify membership by querying pool_members itself — infinite recursion. The Supabase debugger gave a timeout with no clear stack trace. Migration 007 dropped ALL policies on ALL 15 tables and rebuilt from scratch with three strict rules: no table self-references, pool_members uses auth.uid() = user_id for self-access, time and role logic that doesn't belong in SQL lives in API routes. A production-shaped lesson in access-control design.

Cutoff Engine · Deterministic Time Gate

Prediction lock without toss detection

-- Cutoff stored at schedule import: -- cutoff_at = scheduled_at - interval '30 minutes' -- API route enforcement: -- if (now() >= match.cutoff_at) { -- return 403 PREDICTION_WINDOW_CLOSED -- } -- No background job needed. -- No toss detection API needed. -- Server time is authoritative. -- Frontend countdown = display only.

The product rule was simple: predictions lock ~30 minutes before the scheduled start. The implementation matched: store cutoff_at at schedule import, enforce now() < cutoff_at in every prediction API route. No live toss detection cron, no WebSocket subscription for toss events, no polling. Frontend countdowns are display only — malicious clients cannot extend the window. Clear product rule → simple, auditable implementation.

Prize Engine · Nx Formula + Tie Guard

Commission-based pool with rank inversion protection

-- Prize pool: prize_pool = sum(paid_buy_ins) * (1 - commission_pct / 100) -- Nx formula (Top 3 example): -- Parts: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 -- 1st: (3/6) × prize_pool -- 2nd: (2/6) × prize_pool -- 3rd: (1/6) × prize_pool -- Tie: split sum of tied prizes equally -- Rank inversion guard: -- rank R always earns >= rank R+1

Only paid members count toward prize_pool — unpaid members do not inflate the pot. Nx formula gives intuitive splits. The rank inversion guard is the subtle edge case: if positions 1 and 2 tie and split (₹2,250+₹1,500)/2=₹1,875 each, position 3 still gets ₹750 — and the guard ensures no position earns more than a higher rank after multi-way splits. Live preview at pool creation answers the setup-night question before the first member joins.

API Thrift · Free-Tier Constraint as Strategy

One CricketData call per match across all users

-- Schedule: daily cron at 00:30 IST -- ~84 calls for the full IPL season -- Results: on-demand, first user triggers -- INSERT INTO match_results (match_id, ...) -- ON CONFLICT (match_id) DO NOTHING -- → First write wins; race handled by DB -- → Concurrent users? Second write is no-op -- CricketData free tier: 100 calls/day ✓ -- Season total result calls: ~84

CricketData.org's free tier provides 100 calls/day. Schedule runs on a daily Vercel Cron — one call for the whole season's upcoming fixtures. Results are never polled. The first user to open a completed match triggers the fetch; ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING caches it. Every subsequent user reads from the DB. Free-tier limits shaped an architecture that also makes the app fast for the 2nd through Nth user.

Hybrid RLS + API Access Control

Static security floor in DB, dynamic logic in routes

-- RLS handles: static pool isolation -- "Any authenticated user can read pools" -- Prevents unauthenticated DB access -- API routes handle: time-based gates -- "Show picks only after cutoff" -- "Only members see pool data" -- "Only admins can mark paid" -- Why split? -- Time logic in RLS = untestable SQL -- Role checks in routes = debuggable code -- Defence in depth = both layers active

RLS provides a broad security floor — unauthenticated users hit a wall at the database level. But prediction visibility (hide until cutoff), pool membership filtering, and scoring cascades all involve time comparisons and business rules easier to express and debug in application code. Post-Migration 007: RLS for static "who can touch this table" rules; API routes for dynamic "can this specific action happen now." Both layers active, neither redundant.

Screenshot Cards · Growth Designed In

@vercel/og QR embeds on every shareable card

-- Card types (all include QR + join link): -- 1. Leaderboard position (all members) -- 2. "My picks" reveal (all members) -- 3. "Who hasn't predicted" nudge (admin) -- 4. Prize payout card (admin) -- Generation: @vercel/og (satori) -- Server-side → no canvas rendering -- Mobile: navigator.share() native sheet -- Desktop: copy link + download image

Distribution is designed into the UX, not added later. Every screenshot card — whether a member sharing their leaderboard position or an admin posting a payout card — embeds the permanent pool join QR code. Someone who sees the card in a WhatsApp story can join directly from the image. @vercel/og generates cards server-side, so sharing works without client-side canvas rendering. On mobile navigator.share() opens the native sheet; on desktop: copy link and download as image.

Decision Log

Key decisions with reasoning preserved

Curated from March 2026 brainstorming and design phases. Every decision listed here directly shaped a shipped feature or an explicit deferral.

Product Strategy

Skip hypothesis and discovery phases

Problem validated through 3 seasons of manual Google Sheets leagues. No market research needed for a lived-problem family side project. Scope discipline begins at phase zero.

Product Strategy

Primary persona: Pool Admin (not the predictor)

If admin pain is eliminated, pools survive the full season. Admin is also the distribution channel — building for the admin first means the product gets used at all.

Product Strategy

Multi-league architecture at schema level, IPL only at launch

Schema supports any sport from day one (league column, external API abstraction). IPL is the only seeded content. Platform is the long game; IPL is the wedge.

Pools & Membership

One permanent pool link (never per-match)

Per-match links require admin action before every game and create WhatsApp clutter. One pinned link eliminates that permanently. The join flow is always available — the link never expires.

Pools & Membership

Approve ≠ Paid (two separate states)

UPI and cash payments are asynchronous — money doesn't arrive at the same moment as the approval decision. "Approve = you're in" and "Paid = I have your money" map to how real offline payment actually works.

Pools & Membership

Mid-season exit: buy-in stays in the pool (no refund)

The buy-in is an entry fee, not an investment. Contested refund logic adds support burden and creates scope for disputes. Explicit boring policy beats clever logic.

Predictions

No tie option in prediction UI

IPL always has a result — super overs guarantee a winner. Offering a tie option would be factually wrong for the sport. Product matches sport reality.

Predictions

Cutoff = fixed time, not toss detection

Toss detection requires a live API subscription and a cron watching for toss events. Fixed time at schedule import is deterministic, testable, and requires zero live polling. Simpler implementation, same product outcome.

Prizes & Money

Commission % is admin-only — members never see it

Members see prize amounts, not how commission is computed. Showing the percentage creates a "tax" framing that generates resentment. Members care about what they win, not what the admin earns.

Prizes & Money

Never show net losses to members

"Won ₹X" only. Buy-in is a sunk entry fee. Showing that someone is down ₹400 discourages participation in next season and misframes a social competition as an investment vehicle.

Prizes & Money

Money stays offline — app tracks, doesn't process

In-app UPI requires NPCI/RBI registration, payment gateway, refund policy, and support capacity. The MVP boundary: Predictopia tracks settlement; actual money moves via WhatsApp UPI.

Engineering

Migration 007 — nuclear RLS fix (drop all, rebuild from zero)

Rather than patch individual policies and risk hidden recursion chains, dropped ALL policies on all 15 tables and rebuilt with three strict rules: no table self-references, pool_members never queries itself, time/role logic stays in API routes.

Engineering

Synchronous score calculation (no job queue)

At 15 users and 1 pool, scoring 15 predictions takes milliseconds in the request path. Background job infrastructure adds operational complexity with zero user-visible benefit at family scale.

Engineering

Results on-demand, schedule via cron — opposite of the obvious approach

Polling results via cron would require one API call per match per poll interval. On-demand caching requires one call total per match, ever. Free-tier limits force this choice; it also happens to be faster for users.
