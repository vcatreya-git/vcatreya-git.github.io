Every family and friend group already runs a prediction pool.
It just lives in a WhatsApp thread and a dying spreadsheet.

You know the pattern: someone volunteers to "run the pool" every IPL or World Cup. They build a spreadsheet, pin a WhatsApp group, and spend the whole tournament chasing picks, settling arguments about who said what before kickoff, and trying to remember who still owes ₹500. Predictopia is that admin's job, built as software — so the group survives past one season and the ledger survives past one person's memory.

The problem

Running a pool by hand doesn't survive a second season

It starts fine. Round one of the tournament, everyone's in the group chat picking winners, the spreadsheet is up to date, the banter is good. By the semi-finals, three people have missed a match and argue they "said it in the chat," nobody's sure if the buy-in column is current, and the admin is manually tallying who's owed what at 11pm. Next season, the same person has to decide whether it's worth doing it all again — usually starting from a blank sheet, because nothing from last time carried forward.
🔁

The group dies every off-season

A WhatsApp group and a spreadsheet don't persist as a "circle of people who play together" — they persist as one tournament's artifact. Next season means a new invite list, a new sheet, and re-explaining the rules to people who already knew them.

⏱️

No real lock, so every pick is disputable

"I texted my pick before kickoff" is unverifiable in a group chat. Without a hard, visible cutoff, every close call becomes a relitigated argument instead of a settled fact.

💸

Money and points blur into one guess

Who's collected their buy-in and who's owed prize money end up as two columns in the same spreadsheet, edited by hand, understood by exactly one person — the admin — until they make a mistake or lose interest.

How it works

One persistent circle, any number of seasons

Four layers, each solving one part of the spreadsheet problem above. All four are clickable in the prototype today.

1

Group

Your lasting circle — family, office, cousins. Invite once. Admins admit or deny. Survives every future tournament with no re-invite.

2

Pool

One competition under a group (this IPL, this Premier League). Members opt in — no auto-enroll. Admin sets buy-in, periods, prizes.

3

Predict

One-tap outcomes, editable until a real countdown hits zero. Admin specials use the same event model. No "I said it in the chat."

4

Money

Who won what, what you're owed, settled or not — Splitwise for the pool. Cash still moves offline; the app just remembers.

Capabilities

What's in the prototype today

Status reflects the interactive prototype and the independently-reviewed architecture. Production backend is the next phase.

1

Groups that persist

Invite link, admit/deny, co-admins. One circle, unlimited future seasons — no rebuilding next tournament.

2

Opt-in pools

Multiple concurrent pools per group. Nobody is auto-enrolled into a season they don't want to play.

3

One-tap predictions

Cricket win/loss, football Home/Draw/Away, with a visible cutoff countdown — the "I said it in the chat" argument disappears.

4

Cross-pool sync

In more than one pool watching the same competition? Mirror your picks across pools instead of entering them twice.

5

Reveal, after lock

Everyone's picks stay private until cutoff, then unlock on the Board — the banter moment, without letting anyone copy a pick early.

6

Specials

Admin-authored polls ("who makes the final?") on the same event model as match picks — single or multi-select, with resolution.

7

Money, not payments

Owed/Settled for prizes, Collected/Not collected for buy-in — two honest, separate labels. Cash still moves offline; nothing here touches real money.

8

Manage hub, admin-only

Economics, prize splits, and specials live in one admin surface — never leaking onto the member-facing Board.

Who it's for

Three roles, one honest split of responsibility

Every screen is defended by one of these three lenses — nothing exists for a hypothetical fourth user.

Group admin

Owns the circle

Admits members, creates pools, and — because only a group admin can create a pool — runs that season's specials, period close, and settlement. The person who used to own the spreadsheet, minus the spreadsheet.

Co-admin

Delegated, not demoted

Shares Manage-tab duties on a specific pool — pushing specials, marking buy-ins collected — without owning group-membership decisions. Lets one admin actually take a week off during the tournament.

Member

Predicts, checks, gets paid

Picks before cutoff, checks the board, sees exactly what they're owed. Senior-friendly targets and copy — this has to work for the family group, not just the office one.

Technical depth

Reviewed by a second model before any backend code

Drafted with Claude, then independently reviewed by a second model (Grok) — not a rubber stamp. Five required changes came back; all fixed the same day.

!
Security

Privilege-escalation gap in a leave policy

A member's own "leave the group" RLS check only validated user_id, not role — a crafted request could have self-promoted to admin. Fixed with a status-pinning check plus a trigger that blocks any self-service role change. The same gap existed in the pool-leave policy and hadn't been noticed at all — fixed the same pattern.

✓
Correctness

Admin-inheritance gap

A group admin without their own explicit pool-membership row would have been locked out of managing pools they should administer by default — the helper function didn't carry the inheritance rule its own policy already had.

✓
Build safety

A referenced function that was never defined

Period-close cited a leaderboard() function that didn't exist anywhere in the schema — would have failed at build time. Now defined as one SUM() query with a period-type branch.

✓
Scalability

Sports-API rate-limit risk, resolved structurally

Flagged as unvalidated in two earlier product reviews. Architecture confirms calls scale with real-world match count, never with how many pools or groups are predicting on the same match — concrete worst case well under the free-tier cap.
