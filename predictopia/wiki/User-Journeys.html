User Journeys — Predictopia

Narrative journeys that show product judgment: who the user is, what friction they hit, and how Predictopia removes admin labour without becoming a payments app or a gambling product.

Draw.io sources: 06-user-journey-admin.drawio · 07-user-journey-member.drawio · full set in docs/diagrams/

These journeys are the portfolio proof of persona-driven design: every major feature maps to a named person with a seasonal job-to-be-done, not a feature backlog.

Journey 1: Admin sets up the family IPL pool (Day 0)

User: Venkat — pool creator. He organises the family WhatsApp league every year. Historically spends 15–30 minutes per match day on Google Sheets (entry columns, reminders, manual score tallies). Over 70+ IPL matches that is 17–35 hours of unpaid seasonal labour. If he burns out mid-season, the pool dies.

Entry point: Personal link / portfolio / “try it for IPL” message to family.

What happens:

Venkat signs in with Google (no new password — family will not install a separate app store product for cricket banter). Onboarding is three beats: display name, emoji avatar, favourite IPL team. Favourite team is not decoration — it tints nav accents so the product feels “ours” on first open.

He taps Create pool and names it “Family IPL 2026”. He sets:

  • Buy-in ₹500 (real money, offline)
  • Commission 10% (covers trophies / hosting — admin-only visibility later)
  • Distribution Top 3
  • League style that auto-generates weekly/monthly/season competitions

As he adjusts expected headcount, a live prize preview recalculates pot and place amounts. That single surface answers the WhatsApp question that used to dominate setup night: “How much does 1st get?”

He copies the permanent join link and pins it once in the family group. He does not create per-match links. He does not open Sheets. Setup is done.

Product decisions on display:
- One permanent link (zero daily link spam)
- Prize preview before first join (reduces money confusion)
- Money offline — app tracks settlement, does not process UPI (regulatory boundary)
- Commission % never shown to members (avoids “tax” framing fights)

Exit: Pool Home with empty member list waiting for join requests. Admin labour for the rest of the season should collapse to approvals + occasional share nudges.

Success metric: Pool live + link shared in under 10 minutes.

Journey 2: Casual uncle joins and predicts (Match day 1)

User: Ramesh, 58 — wants to play when reminded, not learn a fintech app. Reads WhatsApp every day; will abandon anything with small text or multi-step forms.

Entry: Taps the pinned WhatsApp link on his phone.

What happens:

He signs in with Google (already has Gmail). Pool preview shows name, buy-in ₹500, member count — not the full leaderboard (privacy + conversion: don’t scare him with a table of cousins already predicting).

He requests to join. Venkat approves that evening and marks Paid after UPI lands — two states, because real money is async.

Ramesh opens Predict. Large team tiles (CSK gold / MI blue abbreviations — no copyrighted logos). One tap on the winner. Countdown: “Closes in 2h 15m.” He changes mind once; last pick sticks. After cutoff he sees the reveal: “CSK (8): … · MI (4): …” — content for the group chat, not a spreadsheet.

Next morning the leaderboard shows him mid-table. After three correct in a row a streak badge appears — social proof without a tutorial.

Product decisions on display:
- 48px tap targets and plain language for seniors
- Approve ≠ Paid (offline UPI lag is a first-class state machine)
- Reveal only after cutoff (independent thinking, then banter)
- No tie option (IPL super over — product matches real sport)
- Never show net losses (“Won ₹X” only)

Success metric: First pick before cutoff without calling Venkat for help.

Journey 3: Active predictor across two pools

User: Priya — in family pool and college friends pool. She wants different strategies without two accounts.

What happens:

Same account, My Pools shows two cards with role badges. For the same fixture she predicts CSK in family and MI in college — bragging rights and strategies differ by social graph.

She shares a leaderboard card from the college pool to Instagram Stories. The card embeds a join QR, so every screenshot is still distribution.

Achievements list “Perfect Week” on college pool only — streaks and badges are per-pool, not global vanity that would pollute family standings.

Product decision: multi-pool prediction isolation is a schema requirement (UNIQUE(pool_id, user_id, match_id)), not an afterthought. This is portfolio evidence of multi-tenant thinking at family scale.

Success metric: Two independent predictions for the same match, zero confusion about “which pool am I in?”

Journey 4: Mid-season admin ops (low touch)

User: Venkat again, week 6. The product promise is tested here: if he is back in Sheets, V1 failed.

What happens:

  1. Admin → Requests: two pending joins; approves one, rejects one (pool near full / 100 cap).
  2. Marks three members Paid after weekend UPI.
  3. Shares “Who hasn’t predicted” card into WhatsApp — three people tap through and lock picks before cutoff.
  4. Does not enter scores — result auto-caches when first person opens the finished match (API thrift: one CricketData call per match, ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING).
  5. Checks budget tracker: collected vs allocated vs remaining; commission % stays admin-only.

Product decisions:
- Admin labour collapses to approvals + share nudges
- Results thrifty by design (free-tier API as product constraint)
- Budget transparency without exposing commission % to members
- Admin result override exists for disputes only (source = admin_override)

Success metric: Match-day admin time under 5 minutes; zero spreadsheet opens.

Journey 5: Season end prizes (offline money, in-app truth)

User: Family after the final.

What happens:

Season leaderboard freezes. Prize engine shows Top 3 amounts from the Nx formula (position N from bottom of paid places gets N× share of pot). Venkat pays winners via UPI offline and marks prize rows Paid in app. Members see “Won ₹X” history — never a net-loss guilt UI (buy-in is sunk by design; the product is social competition, not a broker).

Product decision: avoid gambling framing; celebrate winnings; keep settlement human. Portfolio skill: knowing what not to build (payment rails, KYC) is as important as what shipped.

Success metric: Paid marks match offline UPI; zero “the app stole my money” support drama.

Journey 6: Late join (fairness without refunds)

User: Arjun joins mid-season after pool already has 20 completed matches.

What happens:

He sees buy-in and requests join. On approval + paid, late-join policy applies (zero or prorated on pool). Default product stance for family V1: late joiners start from current fixtures; buy-in still full; past points not rewritten inventively. Mid-season exit: buy-in stays in the pool (no refund), predictions preserved for history, points frozen.

Product decision: fairness rules are explicit and boring. Contested refund logic is out of scope for V1 — scope discipline.
