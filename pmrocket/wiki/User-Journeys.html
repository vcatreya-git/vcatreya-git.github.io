User Journeys — PMRocket

Journey 1: First-time setup (The Wanderer)

User: Sarah, a backend engineer exploring PM. She's self-taught for 3 months, built a few side projects, uncertain about fit.

Entry point: Lands on PMRocket from a friend's recommendation.

What happens:

Sarah clicks "Get started" and takes a 3-minute intake. Fifteen questions flow one at a time on her phone during lunch break:

  • "What's your background?" — She picks "Software Engineer"
  • "How many years?" — 5 years
  • "Have you shipped features?" — Yes, multiple
  • "Have you run user interviews?" — Never
  • "How comfortable are you with databases?" — Very comfortable
  • "How comfortable are you with go-to-market?" — Not at all
  • "What excites you?" — Building developer tools
  • "What company type?" — Startup or scale-up
  • "What geography?" — North America

After 15 signals, the system displays her match: Technical PM, 78% confidence.

Sarah sees the reasoning: strong technical background (rare in PM candidates), shipped code (relevant), but zero GTM experience (a gap). The system shows her adjacent options—she could target Platform PM instead, or upskill in growth to be a stronger Technical PM. For now, Technical PM feels right.

She clicks "See my roadmap" and is taken to her personalised preparation path.

Journey 2: Building evidence (3-month arc)

User: Sarah, same, now 1 month into using PMRocket.

What happens:

Sarah sees her roadmap—65 artefacts organized in three layers:

  • Universal layer (11 artefacts): Customer Interviews, Business Metrics 101, Shipping Roadmaps, etc. These apply to all PMs.
  • Technical PM layer (18 artefacts): System Design Trade-offs, Technical Debt Roadmaps, Architecting for Scale, Working with Research Teams.
  • Developer Tools domain (12 artefacts): API Design Excellence, Developer Onboarding Flows, CLI UX Patterns.

She starts with "Customer Interviews" under Universal. The artefact has a description: "Conduct and document 5+ user interviews with target users. Capture pain points, workflows, unmet needs. This demonstrates customer empathy and insight gathering—table stakes for all PMs."

The artefact is broken into progressive tiers:

  • Learning (foundational): Watch 2 interview videos, read the interview guide template, conduct 1 interview
  • Practised (applied): Conduct 3 interviews, write a synthesis doc, identify themes
  • Mastered (expert): Conduct 5+ interviews, build a user journey map, defend your findings in a mock meeting

Sarah ticks off boxes as she goes. She watches the videos (✓). She reads the template (✓). She schedules her first interview and realizes she needs help with the script. She adds a note: "Used the PMRocket script template from the linked deck—much better than my first attempt."

A week later, she comes back and ticks "conducted 1 interview (✓)". The system shows: "Learning tier 20% complete. Keep going!"

She keeps working. By week 3, she's done 4 interviews, written a synthesis, and is about to unlock Practised. The system shows a celebration: "🎉 Learning tier complete! You've built a foundation in customer research. Next up: Practised tier—deeper synthesis and defense."

Sarah clicks through to Practised and ticks "conduct 3 interviews (✓)" since she's already done more than that. The system updates to "Practised 75% complete."

Over the next 3 months, Sarah repeats this process for 12 more artefacts. Her dashboard shows progress: 13 artefacts started, 3 at Mastered, 7 at Practised, 3 still Learning. Her readiness score climbs from 22 (just from taking the intake) to 61 (solid progress).

She's building real proof. A shipped feature becomes a case study on GitHub. An interview study becomes a Notion doc she can link to. A strategy doc she wrote at work becomes portfolio evidence. All of it logged in one place.

Journey 3: The readiness moment

User: Sarah, same, now 6 months into PMRocket.

What happens:

Sarah's dashboard shows a readiness score of 78. The breakdown by skill shows she's strongest in Technical Fluency (94), solid in Portfolio Evidence (82), weaker in Business Impact (61).

She sees a "Gap Analysis" button she hasn't clicked before. It says: "See how your evidence stacks up against real Technical PM job descriptions."

She clicks it. The system shows her the analysis:

  • Top gap #1: "Business metrics and data storytelling." 87% of Technical PM jobs for developer tools mention this. You have Customer Interviews and a Shipped Product, but no evidence of running metrics dashboards or presenting data stories.
  • Gap #2: "Roadmap prioritisation and trade-offs." 72% of jobs mention this. You have System Design Trade-offs, but no shipped roadmap or prioritisation decision doc from a real project.
  • Gap #3: "Cross-functional communication." 61% of jobs mention this. You've shipped features but no evidence of coordinating across design/eng/marketing.

The system recommends three artefacts:
- "Build a metrics dashboard" (Portfolio Evidence layer) — would close Gap #1 fastest
- "Document 3 hard trade-off decisions" (Technical PM layer) — would close Gap #2
- "Run a cross-functional kickoff" (Universal layer) — would close Gap #3

The readiness gate: Sarah has hit 78/100 readiness, which is above the "ready to apply" threshold of 75. The system shows a checkmark:

✓ You're ready to apply for Technical PM roles in developer tools.

This isn't arbitrary. The gate is built from her evidence and the market data. She's not ready to apply for Growth PM roles (she'd need 6 more months of metrics work), and she's not ready for Enterprise PM (no sales experience). But for Technical PM in developer tools? The evidence backs it.

Journey 4: Archetype switch

User: Marco, takes intake and gets matched to Enterprise PM.

What happens:

Marco asks his friend: "Could I be a Technical PM instead? That sounds cooler."

Marco's friend tells him: "Click your name at the top, go to Profile, and you can try a different archetype."

Marco clicks through. He sees his current match: Enterprise PM, 82%. The system shows him adjacent archetypes:
- Technical PM (68%) — "You have low technical depth but some infrastructure experience"
- Growth PM (71%) — "Good data skills but limited product analytics"

Marco clicks "Try Technical PM" to see that roadmap. The system takes him to the Technical PM match screen, shows him the reasoning, and gives him the option to switch.

If Marco switches, his intake stays the same—the system just shows him a different roadmap. His evidence log resets for Technical PM (he hasn't built any Technical PM proof yet, even though he has artefacts that might transfer). This is intentional: evidence is archetype-specific. Marco's enterprise deal story doesn't count as Technical PM proof until he builds technical system design chops.

Marco realizes Enterprise PM is actually a better fit. He goes back to his original roadmap.

Journey 5: The ready-to-apply flow (V3a, future)

User: Sarah, same, 6 months in, now ready to apply.

What happens (not yet shipped):

Sarah is ready to apply for her first Technical PM role. She finds a job posting from a developer tools company she loves.

She comes to PMRocket and clicks "Apply" → "Paste job description". She copies the JD from the company website and pastes it.

The system extracts the key requirements from the JD and compares them against her logged evidence and interview profile:

  • Strong match: "System design and architectural thinking" — you have 3 Mastered artefacts here
  • Good match: "Shipped products to real users" — you have 5 shipped features documented
  • Weak match: "Data-driven prioritisation" — you have Customer Interviews but no metrics dashboard

The system then drafts a targeted resume from Sarah's evidence:

Technical Product Manager — Developer Tools

Sarah brings 5+ years of software engineering experience shipping 
complex systems at scale, translating that technical depth into 
product leadership. She has led three major architectural decisions 
in production systems, documented in [GitHub link]. She has 
validated product direction through 12+ customer interviews across 
3 developer tools domains, synthesized into [case study link].

Key technical areas: system design, scaling challenges, infrastructure 
trade-offs, API design. Areas for growth: metrics-driven decision making, 
go-to-market positioning.

The resume includes only artefacts Sarah has actually completed, with direct links to proof. No invented experience. No keyword stuffing.

The system also surfaces an action plan: "Before applying, consider building one Metrics Dashboard artifact to close your strongest gap. This JD emphasizes data storytelling in 5 of 8 job bullets."

Sarah has the choice: apply now (she's above the readiness threshold), or spend 2 weeks closing her strongest gap. She chooses to build the dashboard first. Good product doesn't rush people to apply before they're confident.

Journey 6: Coming back (retention)

User: Sarah, 12 months into PMRocket, just accepted a Technical PM role.

What happens (not yet shipped—V2b feature):

Sarah's first week on the job, she's drowning. New codebase, new org, new team dynamics. She remembers her PMRocket profile and opens the app.

She's no longer an aspiring PM—she's a PM. But she's new to this domain and this company type. She clicks "Update my status" in her profile and changes from "Aspiring PM" to "Working PM - new to role".

Her dashboard now shows a different set of suggestions:

  • "You have Technical Fluency but no experience with this company's deployment complexity. Recommended: System Migration Roadmaps (Practised tier)"
  • "You shipped features, but not in this domain. Recommended: Domain expertise artefact for your specific vertical"

Her readiness score is now recalculated for "new PM in this domain" rather than "ready to apply". She has a new navigation path.

Over the next 6 months, Sarah uses PMRocket to track her progression from day-one onboarding to confident leadership in her domain. The same product that helped her navigate to her role now helps her navigate within her role.
