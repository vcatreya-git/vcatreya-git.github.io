The problem

The navigation gap every aspiring PM faces

Aspiring PMs are drowning in generic content. Bootcamps teach frameworks to everyone equally. Communities celebrate "become a PM" without asking which PM. Candidates build the wrong things, on the wrong timeline, toward the wrong goal. The deeper problem is the absence of a feedback loop. You prepare in the dark. Bootcamp alumni confidently apply before they have shipped anything. Self-taught learners spend a year on case studies but never validate whether a company would care. The system has no signal for readiness — just silence.
🧭

Wrong direction

An aspiring engineer wanting to build products needs different evidence than someone from consulting moving into PM. A growth PM candidate in fintech faces a different technical bar than one in consumer. Existing tools treat them identically — no archetype matching, no personalisation based on background. Candidates prepare generically for roles that don't fit them.

📊

No market-grounded readiness signal

Existing readiness scores use invented internal rubrics with no connection to what employers actually screen for. Bootcamp alumni confidently apply before they have shipped anything. Self-taught learners spend a year on case studies but never validate whether a company would care. Candidates have no way to know if what they've built is what the market actually wants.

🚪

No honest gate

There is no point at which the system tells you "you're ready." Users apply prematurely, get rejected, and don't know why. The system has no signal for readiness — just silence. PMRocket is that gate — grounded in real JD data, not an invented rubric. When gap ≤ 30% with no critical missing skills, the system tells you you're ready. Not before.

Platform capabilities

What the platform does

Eight capabilities across the seven-stage pipeline. Capabilities 1–5 are live. 6–8 are on the roadmap.

1

Weighted archetype matching

Live

Every intake answer is a signal for every PM archetype. A software engineering background scores highest for Technical PM but also carries signal for AI/Platform PM. The model uses configurable weights stored in the database — not editorial opinion. You get a primary archetype with confidence % and a runner-up with reasoning. No two users get the same result unless their backgrounds are genuinely identical.

2

Three-layer personalised curriculum

Live

Layer 1 (Universal) covers AI literacy and core PM skills every archetype needs. Layer 2 (Archetype) covers skills specific to your match — Technical PM sees system design; Growth PM sees experimentation frameworks. Layer 3 (Domain × Geography) covers requirements specific to your target market and industry. A Technical PM in fintech in North America gets different content from one in India (Dodd-Frank vs RBI requirements).

3

Progressive evidence tiers

Live

Each artefact has three tiers: Learning, Practised, Mastered. Tiers unlock as you check off sub-topics — no manual promotion. Each artefact has 15 sub-topics split into three equal groups and 5 targeted search keywords to guide what to study. The system celebrates each tier unlock with an animation and shows exactly where you're strong and where you have gaps. Score updates synchronously on every log.

4

Market-grounded gap analysis

V2a — building

The system retrieves the top 25 most similar job descriptions from the corpus using pgvector KNN (cosine similarity, HNSW index). It calculates gap % per skill area by comparing your evidence against JD requirements. An LLM generates a plain-English narrative identifying your top 3 gaps with specific artefacts that would close them fastest. Hidden until 15+ JDs exist for your slice — no false signals from thin data.

5

Readiness score across five dimensions

Live

Score is weighted across Technical (30%), Business (25%), Portfolio (25%), Domain (10%), and Product (10%). It auto-updates when you log evidence — no manual refresh. The radar chart on the Readiness page visualises the breakdown by category. The gate is honest: score ≥ 75 and gap ≤ 30% with no critical missing skills before the system tells you you're ready. Not before.

6

Admin ETL pipeline

Live

Admin pastes a job description. LLM extracts skill requirements and maps them to the existing taxonomy vocabulary — it cannot invent new skill terms. Admin reviews and approves each skill in a queue UI. System embeds and indexes. No code deployment required to update the curriculum or retune matching weights. The Curriculum Signals panel surfaces Trending (high JD frequency, not in curriculum), Stable, and Declining skills.

7

Evidence-backed resume generation

V3a — planned

Once the readiness gate is reached, the user pastes a specific job description. The system scores their evidence against that JD's requirements, surfaces the strongest matches and any remaining gaps, and generates a resume built entirely from logged evidence. Each bullet links to real proof the user has documented. No keyword stuffing. No invented experience. Every claim is traceable to a logged artefact.

8

AI interview room + job matching

V3b — planned

Mock interviews specific to the user's archetype, domain, and the evidence they have logged. Feedback is tied to how the user would present their actual proof in an interview — not generic PM coaching. Paired with job matching: surfaces open roles that match the user's current evidence level, not just their aspirations. You compete on what you've built, not what you say you can build.

Journey 1 — First-time setup: The Wanderer

Sarah · backend engineer · 3 months self-directed prep · uncertain about fit · entry via friend's recommendation

Sarah clicks "Get started" and takes a 3-minute intake. Fifteen questions flow one at a time on her phone during lunch:

"What's your background?" → Software Engineer
"How many years?" → 5 years
"Have you shipped features?" → Yes, multiple
"Have you run user interviews?" → Never
"How comfortable are you with databases?" → Very comfortable
"How comfortable are you with go-to-market?" → Not at all
"What excites you?" → Building developer tools
"What company type?" → Startup or scale-up
"What geography?" → North America
Technical PM · 78% confidence · Runner-up: Growth PM (64%)

Sarah sees the reasoning: strong technical background (rare in PM candidates), shipped code (relevant), but zero GTM experience — a gap, not a disqualifier. The system shows adjacent options: she could target Platform PM instead, or upskill in growth to be a stronger Technical PM candidate. Technical PM feels right.

She clicks "See my roadmap." The product now knows her archetype, domain (developer tools), geography (North America), and skill profile. Everything from here is personalised to those signals.

Journey 2 — Building evidence: 3-month arc

Sarah · 1 month in · roadmap unlocked · first artefact started

Sarah sees her roadmap — artefacts organised in three layers:

  • Universal layer: Customer Interviews, Business Metrics 101, Shipping Roadmaps — applies to all PMs
  • Technical PM layer: System Design Trade-offs, Technical Debt Roadmaps, Architecting for Scale
  • Developer Tools domain: API Design Excellence, Developer Onboarding Flows, CLI UX Patterns

She starts with "Customer Interviews." The artefact has 15 sub-topics tiered across Learning, Practised, and Mastered — with 5 Google-ready search keywords to guide what to study. She ticks sub-topics as she works: reads the interview guide template (✓), watches the technique video (✓), schedules her first interview. A week later she ticks "conducted 1 interview (✓)". By week 3 she's done 4 interviews and unlocks Practised tier — the animation fires, the card glows green, her score bumps 12 points.

Over 3 months she completes 12 more artefacts. Her readiness score climbs from 22 to 61. Logged in one place: a shipped feature as a GitHub case study, an interview study as a Notion doc, a strategy memo from work. Real proof, not invented. Every log under 90 seconds.

Journey 3 — The readiness moment

Sarah · 6 months in · readiness score 78 · gap analysis triggered for the first time

Her dashboard shows readiness score 78. Breakdown by dimension: Technical Fluency 94, Portfolio Evidence 82, Business Impact 61, Domain Knowledge 74, Product Thinking 68.

She clicks "Gap Analysis" for the first time. The system retrieves the 25 most similar Technical PM jobs in developer tools in North America and shows:

  • Top gap #1: "Business metrics and data storytelling" — 87% of Technical PM jobs in developer tools mention this. She has Customer Interviews and a Shipped Product but no metrics dashboard evidence.
  • Gap #2: "Roadmap prioritisation and trade-offs" — 72% of jobs. She has System Design Trade-offs but no shipped roadmap doc.
  • Gap #3: "Cross-functional communication" — 61%. She's shipped features but has no evidence of coordinating across design, engineering, and marketing.

The system recommends three specific artefacts to close each gap — with search keywords and a description of what "Mastered" looks like for each.

✓ You're ready to apply for Technical PM roles in developer tools.

This isn't arbitrary. The gate is grounded in her evidence and the market data. Score 78 (threshold: 75). Gap 23% (threshold: 30%). No critical missing skills. She's not ready for Growth PM — would need 6 more months of metrics work. Not for Enterprise PM — no sales experience. But for Technical PM in developer tools? The evidence backs it.

Journey 4 — Archetype switch: Marco

Marco · matched to Enterprise PM (82%) · exploring whether Technical PM is a better fit

Marco clicks his name, goes to Profile, and sees his alternative archetypes: Technical PM (68%) — "You have low technical depth but some infrastructure experience" and Growth PM (71%) — "Good data skills but limited product analytics experience."

He clicks "Try Technical PM" to see that roadmap. The system shows him the reasoning — his Q12 (technical skills self-rating: 4/10) scored only 0.4 for Technical PM versus 0.9 for Enterprise PM. The Technical PM roadmap would require 14 additional artefacts he hasn't started. Enterprise PM roadmap has 3 artefacts left.

If Marco switches, his intake stays the same — the system shows a different roadmap. His evidence log is archetype-specific: his enterprise deal story doesn't count as Technical PM proof until he builds system design chops. Switching would reset his readiness score from 71 to 31.

Marco realises Enterprise PM is actually the better fit. He goes back. The system doesn't punish the exploration — it shows the cost clearly and lets him decide.

Who it's for

Built for two distinct users

Every feature decision is defended through the lens of a specific persona. No feature creep from hypothetical users. Scope is defended by these lenses, not by feature lists.

Primary · V1 Live

The Wanderer

Aspiring PM · 3–6 months into self-directed preparation · stuck without direction

Has read the books, watched the talks, built some case studies. Does not know if she is building the right things for the right role. Does not know when to start applying. PMRocket tells her: here is your archetype, here is what matters for your domain and market, here is what you still need.

The Wanderer is narrow on purpose. She's not "aspiring PM" broadly — she's someone already 3–6 months into prep who is stuck without direction. This specificity scopes every feature decision: the intake is 15 questions (not 30), the roadmap shows artefacts (not content), the readiness signal is market-grounded (not a course completion %). Every feature is explained by this persona.

Secondary · V2a Planned

The Navigator

Existing PM · levelling up, switching domain, or moving to a new market

Has proven she can do PM but needs to prove competence in a new area. PMRocket becomes a career progression tool: maps the specific gaps between where she is and where she wants to be, using market data as the ground truth.

The match algorithm gives less weight to background signals (Q1–Q4) because they already know their PM type. Q17 (current level: IC1/IC2/Staff/Director) and Q18 (transition goal: level up/switch domain/switch archetype) carry more weight. Gap analysis retrieves JDs at the target seniority level — an IC2 targeting IC3 sees Senior PM JDs, not entry-level. Score computation recognises existing PM experience and doesn't force re-learning foundational artefacts.

Technical depth

Under the hood

8 production-grade design decisions. Not a toy demo — every layer has a reason.

Scoring model

Weighted archetype matching

score(A) = Σ[w(q,A) × raw(q,A,answer)] ───────────────────────────── Σ w(q,A)

The model treats every intake response as a scored signal for every PM archetype. Fifteen signals are grouped into five categories — background, experience, skills, domain, goals — and each carries a configurable weight per archetype stored in a question_weights table. A software engineer answering Q1 scores 1.0 for Technical PM and 0.3 for Consumer PM; the same Q1 for a finance background inverts those scores. Q12 (technical skills self-rating) carries the highest weight (9) for Technical PM and Platform archetypes. Tie-breaking uses Q12 absolute contribution when two archetypes score within 0.02 of each other. Weights are admin-configurable at runtime — no code deployment required.

Evidence tiers

Progressive artefact tier unlocking

topic_progress JSONB → completion % 0–33% → Learning unlocked 34–66% → Practised unlocked 67–100% → Mastered unlocked

When a user logs evidence for an artefact, the system splits the artefact's key_topics[] into three equal-size tiers (Learning/Practised/Mastered). The evidence modal presents progressive checkboxes for each tier; completing all checkboxes in Learning automatically unlocks Practised. No manual tier progression logic — tier structure is derived from topic completion percentages. The topic_progress JSONB column tracks which topics the user has marked across all evidence logs for an artefact. Tier animations celebrate promotion and unlock premium features.

Score computation

Readiness score with self-healing

computeAndSaveScore(userId) → rubrics × tier multiplier (0.5/0.8/1.0) → UPSERT readiness_scores → self-heals on GET if row missing

computeAndSaveScore(userId) aggregates evidence, applies per-category rubrics (Technical artefacts worth 8 pts each, Portfolio artefacts worth 5 pts each), multiplies by completion tier (Learning = 0.5×, Practised = 0.8×, Mastered = 1.0×), and saves to readiness_scores via UPSERT. When a user logs evidence (POST /api/evidence-log), the route awaits score computation synchronously — no fire-and-forget callbacks. The readiness GET endpoint is self-healing: if no score row exists, it computes on demand. Prevents stale data even if the webhook or async job fails.

RAG pipeline

pgvector HNSW gap analysis

text-embedding-3-small (1536d) → HNSW (m=16, ef=64) → K=25 → LLM narrative gap_pct = (1 − evidenced/total) × 100

Gap analysis uses a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline against a live corpus of embedded job descriptions. The system embeds the user's profile (archetype + domain + geography + skill signal) and queries jd_embeddings via pgvector cosine similarity with K=25 retrieval. Retrieved JDs are passed with the user's evidence log to the LLM, which produces a qualitative narrative and identifies the top three skill gaps with recommended artefacts. gap_pct is deterministic and computed separately from the LLM narrative. The gap analysis button is hidden if fewer than 15 JDs exist for the user's slice — the product never generates a signal from an insufficient corpus.

Provider factory

LLM abstraction layer

ACTIVE_LLM_PROVIDER=groq|anthropic|google getLLMModel() → Vercel AI SDK instance getEmbedModel() → same pattern

All LLM calls — ETL skill extraction and gap analysis narrative — route through a factory pattern that decouples the application from any specific provider. getLLMModel() reads the ACTIVE_LLM_PROVIDER env var and returns the appropriate Vercel AI SDK model instance: Groq (Llama 3.3 70B, free tier, default), Anthropic (Claude Sonnet 4.6), or Google (Gemini 2.0 Flash). One env var change swaps providers — zero code changes. Groq runs inference free at portfolio scale; the abstraction means the same code runs in production on Anthropic or Google without modification.

Editorial engine

Admin ETL pipeline

JD paste → PII strip → LLM extract (vocab-constrained to skill_frequency) → admin approve/reject per skill → embed → jd_embeddings → skill_frequency recalculation

The admin surface keeps the curriculum market-grounded without code deployment. An admin pastes raw JD text; the system calls the LLM extraction endpoint, which strips PII and maps each skill to the existing taxonomy vocabulary — the LLM cannot introduce new skill terms, only map to known entries. The admin reviews each extraction in a queue UI. On approval, the system embeds the full JD and triggers POST /api/aggregate-skill-frequency to recalculate frequencies for the affected slice. The Curriculum Signals panel surfaces Trending, Stable, and Declining skills with one-click actions.

Schema management

Node.js migration runner

scripts/migrate.ts → @neondatabase/serverless → migrations table → compare applied set → run each in tx → failure rolls back → halts with clear error

The database schema is managed by 18 pure PostgreSQL SQL files applied in numeric order via a custom Node.js runner at scripts/migrate.ts. No Supabase CLI dependency, no ORM, no framework magic. The runner connects to Neon via @neondatabase/serverless, creates a migrations table on first run to track applied filenames, compares against the applied set, and runs each unapplied migration inside a transaction — failure rolls back and halts the runner with a clear error. The same runner works against any PostgreSQL-compatible database, which matters for the planned Vercel → AWS ECS Fargate transition.

Auth architecture

Clerk TEXT user IDs

users.id TEXT (not UUID) format: user_2abc... webhook → provision users + user_profiles + subscriptions (single tx) lazy provision on every auth'd route

The V3 architecture uses Clerk for auth, which required a non-obvious schema change: users.id is TEXT, not UUID. Clerk user IDs follow the format user_2abc... — they are not UUIDs. Every foreign key referencing users.id across the schema is therefore TEXT. User rows are provisioned via a Clerk user.created webhook that inserts into users, user_profiles, and subscriptions in a single transaction. To handle late or failed webhooks, every authenticated API route includes a lazy provision check: if the user row is missing on a valid Clerk session, it is created before the business logic runs.

What each phase delivers, what's explicitly excluded, and why the sequencing is locked. Every deferred feature is placed in a phase — nothing is "maybe later."

Alpha Live now

What it does

Intake + archetype match + personalised roadmap + evidence logging + readiness score.

Why this phase

Validates the core navigation insight — does matching work? Do users build evidence? Does the readiness signal resonate? All features free to remove adoption friction and focus on signal.

In scope

  • 15-question intake (Wanderer path only; Navigator deferred)
  • Weighted archetype matching across 15 signals
  • Seeded 3-layer curriculum (one domain, two archetypes for demo)
  • Evidence logging with progressive tiers
  • Readiness score via curriculum coverage
  • Archetype switcher to test match confidence

Out of scope for Alpha

  • Multiple geography slices (North America only)
  • RAG gap analysis (insufficient JD corpus)
  • Paywall enforcement (free for portfolio)
  • Navigator path (existing PMs)
  • Interview practice, coaching, resume builder
V2a — Market-grounded intelligence Building next

What it does

Gap analysis grounded in real job descriptions. The system reads your evidence against 80+ actual job postings for your role and tells you the three biggest gaps with recommended artefacts to close them.

Why after Alpha

Alpha must prove the navigation works before adding intelligence. Gap analysis is only valuable once users have logged evidence and the curriculum is trusted.

Sequencing logic

Intelligence must come before Apply packaging. You can't build an honest resume without knowing the gaps. You can't practice interviews without knowing what the market cares about.

In scope

  • Expanded curriculum (all archetypes, multiple domains, NA + India)
  • ETL pipeline: paste JD → LLM extract → admin review → embed
  • RAG gap analysis: match profile to 80+ JDs, retrieve top 25, identify gaps
  • Ready-to-apply gate: gap ≤ 30% + no critical missing skills
  • Admin ETL interface: paste, extract, review, approve
  • Paywall enforcement: match free, roadmap + gap paid
V3a — Package proof for a target job Planned

What it does

Paste a specific job description. Get a resume draft built from your logged evidence, re-framed for that JD.

Why here

Only makes sense after gap analysis exists. You draft a resume once you know the specific gaps between you and the role.

In scope

  • Paste target JD or upload PDF
  • Extract JD requirements
  • Generate resume sections using logged evidence
  • Highlight strong areas, call out weak areas honestly
  • Export as PDF

Out of scope

  • ATS keyword stuffing
  • Auto-apply
  • Multi-job campaign management
V3b — Practice and discover Planned

What it does

Interview room for mock practice. Job matching to find open roles aligned with your evidence level.

Why here

Practice is only valuable after you've packaged your proof. Mock interview feedback should be specific to how you'd present your evidence — not generic PM coaching. Job matching surfaces roles you're actually ready for, not just roles you aspire to.

In scope

  • AI mock interview room (archetype-specific questions)
  • Feedback tied to actual logged evidence
  • Job matching (roles you're evidence-ready for)
Product thinking

Key product decisions

The choices that shaped this product — and why each was made this way and not another. These are the decisions that best demonstrate product thinking: problem framing, trade-off reasoning, and scope discipline.

2026-07-28

Evidence enrichment: two-step model, not front-loaded fields

Adding context, impact, and scale fields to the evidence log modal introduces friction at the learning phase — the most fragile engagement point. A dedicated "enrich for resume" step triggers only when a user approaches the readiness gate (Mastered tier on key artefacts). The LLM can also surface thin entries and prompt for detail at V3a resume draft time. Logging stays low-friction; richness is captured when motivation is highest.

2026-07-28

Primary market: North America first, India second

Builder uses the product personally in North America. India remains a core market for product design and corpus — but North America seeds first. Supersedes India-only primary framing in earlier documents. Corpus seeding, JD retrieval, and geography-specific curriculum start with NA slices.

2026-07-18

Build strategy: thin vertical slice before full V2a surface

One geography × domain × archetype slice runnable end-to-end is more valuable than partial V2a features across many slices. Full V2a docs remain design targets, not the next sprint backlog. A working Technical PM × Fintech × North America slice proves the pipeline before expanding.

2026-07-18

Stripe / live paywall deferred for Alpha

Invite-only or flag-gated access is sufficient for personal use and portfolio demo. Paywall boundary is fully designed and present in the product; live charges are deferred until real traction. This avoids Stripe compliance overhead during a solo exploration phase.

2026-07-18

Production-grade SaaS craft; free-tier runtime until traction

Portfolio goal is to demonstrate PM-level product thinking and production build quality — not a toy demo. Free tiers govern cost and runtime only. Auth, ownership checks, migrations, error handling, paywall design, admin ops, and honest readiness metrics are always present. The roadmap shows a clear transition path to AWS when traction justifies it.

2026-03-24

Paywall boundary: match free, roadmap + gap analysis paid

Free: intake + archetype match + teaser ("you have X artefacts, Y gaps — locked"). Paid: full evidence roadmap, gap analysis, evidence log, readiness signal. Match result alone is useless without the roadmap — the gap creates genuine pull toward paid. This mirrors the classic freemium insight mechanic: show the problem, charge for the solution.

2026-03-24

Navigator path deferred to V2a.1

Q16 ("Are you currently a PM?") ships in V2a for data collection only. Q17–Q19 (current level, transition goal, skill focus), level-filtered JD retrieval, and goal-adjusted weights arrive in V2a.1. Reduces 5+ conditional code paths from the V2a critical path. Accelerate if >20% of V2a users answer Q16 = Yes.

2026-03-24

Minimum Viable Corpus: 4 slices, 80 JDs at Alpha

Technical PM + Enterprise PM × Fintech + B2B SaaS × India = 4 slices × 20 JDs. Gap analysis is hidden for unseeded slices with an honest placeholder ("gap analysis not yet available for your slice"). Expand to Growth PM and Consumer domain post-Alpha based on demand, not calendar.

2026-03-24

ETL vocabulary constraint

LLM extraction prompt includes the existing skill_frequency.skill_name vocabulary list. Prevents skill name fragmentation in frequency counts — "stakeholder management" and "stakeholder comms" don't become separate entries. New skills surface as outliers for admin review rather than auto-creating new taxonomy entries.

2026-03-25

Admin is a decision queue, not a workbench

Admin interface surfaces system recommendations; admin accepts or rejects. No manual weight research, no direct JD paste workflow that requires expertise. Every admin screen shows a recommendation with Accept/Reject. The system originates the analysis; the admin governs it. This scales admin work without growing headcount.

2026-03-22

Product territory: navigation layer, not content

PMRocket must own background-to-role matching + personalised roadmap + readiness signal. Building another content or curriculum platform enters saturated territory (Exponent, NextLeap, Lenny's) with no differentiation. Content is a substitutable commodity; navigation is the moat. The discovery phase validated this as the unmet need.

2026-03-23

Business model: B2C-first

Pain is individual, not institutional. Existing candidates already pay individually for prep products (Exponent at $12/mo, bootcamps at $2K+). B2B institutional buyer (MBA programs, bootcamps) has misaligned incentive metrics — they measure completion and reviews, not readiness accuracy. B2B is Phase 2, only if B2C-first is proven.

2026-07-19

Feature sequencing: Intelligence → Apply → Practice → Channels

Evidence is the source of truth. ETL + RAG gap must exist before job packaging (Apply). Interview practice only makes sense after resume is packaged — feedback must be specific to how you'd present your evidence. Coaching channels last — channels without value delivery are overhead. This sequencing defends against feature creep.
